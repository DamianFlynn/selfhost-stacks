<objective>
Turn a one-time proof into standing coverage, and prove the coverage fails closed.

Phases 1 and 2 both closed by folding a check into `scripts/quick-health-check.sh` (D-29, D-41).
D-20 continues that. It is the difference between a phase that fixed something and a phase that will
notice when the fix comes undone — and this phase exists precisely because nothing noticed for
36 hours while `/` went to zero.

Two things this plan does that the fold-in itself does not:

**It executes the negative controls.** D-19 says an unreachable LXC 100, an unreachable atlantis, a
stopped Jellyfin and an unmounted dataset are all **failures**, not skips — the convention
`check-music-consumers.sh` already uses, and the reason it exists is that the estate's worst outage
was six weeks of silence being read as clean. Reading the fail-closed branches and agreeing they look
right does not discharge VALIDATION.md rows 21-24. They must be run, with the check pointed at an
unroutable address and at a container name that does not exist, and the exit codes recorded.

**It records what is not covered.** D-22 keeps `quick-health-check.sh` **manual** and requires the gap
be written down. Scheduling and alerting is a real capability with its own delivery, deduplication and
channel decisions — and Phase 2 closed with a mount-failure notification that was **never proven to
deliver**. An unproven notification path is worse than a known-manual check, because it feels covered.

The fold-in's own trap is already documented twice: the WR-09 defect, where the consuming block
anchors on `^📊 N\. Summary` and a renumbering silently breaks it while the branch that prints a tick
still runs. Both existing fold-ins now treat a missing anchor as UNKNOWN rather than green. The third
must too, and this plan proves it by breaking the anchor on purpose.

Purpose: TRAN-05's standing, fail-closed detection — the thing that would have caught the incident
before `/` reached zero.
Output: a third fold-in block, four executed negative controls, and the manual-only limit on record.
</objective>
