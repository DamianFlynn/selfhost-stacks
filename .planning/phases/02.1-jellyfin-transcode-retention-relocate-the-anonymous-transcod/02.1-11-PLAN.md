<objective>
Close **GAP 1 (CR-01)**: ROADMAP Success Criterion 6 claims `scripts/check-jellyfin-transcode.sh`
*asserts* "the transcode quota **and the five encoding values**". It does not — lines ~562-573 print
all five through `info()`, so `FAILURES` never increments on drift, and
`scripts/quick-health-check.sh:332`'s selector omits them entirely, so on the green path they are
neither asserted nor displayed.

The consequence is specific and cheap to close: `TranscodingTempPath` is the single setting that
decides whether the 50 G ZFS quota bounds anything at all. Set it to Jellyfin's own default
`/config/transcodes` and the transcode cache moves onto `fast/appdata`, off the quota'd dataset —
while the binds, both mount instruments and the `/` headroom floor all stay green and the script
prints `✅ Jellyfin transcode retention intact`. That directly contradicts this phase's central
claim (ROADMAP Goal) that "a standing fail-closed check would have caught the incident before `/`
reached zero" for the exact failure mode the incident took.

**THE SC6 TENSION IS RESOLVED HERE, EXPLICITLY, AND THE RESOLUTION IS: ASSERT ALL FIVE.**
The verification report offered two ways out — assert all five, or assert two and reword SC6 down.
All five is chosen, for four reasons stated so nobody re-opens it:

1. **The CONS-04 objection does not apply to any of the five.** The existing in-code justification
   ("a standing check cannot prove a setting FIRES") is sound but answers a *different question*.
   Comparing `SegmentKeepSeconds` to `300` is the same class of operation as comparing
   `TranscodingTempPath` to `/cache/transcodes`: a deterministic read-back of a value 02.1-05 wrote.
   It needs no transcode, no API mutation and no log. Asserting two and reporting three would draw
   the line in a place the mechanism does not support.
2. **Asserting two leaves a second, subtler version of the same defect.** `SegmentKeepSeconds`
   drifting to `86400` — a plausible UI edit — silently deletes the retention policy this phase
   installed, and under a two-value scheme the check stays green. Same shape as CR-01, one field
   over.
3. **The 01-09 permanently-red trap is handled by construction.** Every expectation is a named
   constant in one block, `${VAR:-default}`-overridable. A deliberate policy change is a one-line
   edit and the red names the exact line to change.
4. **It keeps ROADMAP SC6's substantive commitment rather than reducing it.** SC6 still gets a
   wording amendment, but a *clarifying* one: the check asserts the five values have not **drifted**,
   and criterion 4 remains the sole firing proof. That closes the ambiguity the verifier flagged
   without weakening what was promised. Reducing "five" to "two" would be a scope reduction of a
   ROADMAP commitment, which is not this plan's to make.

Reconciled at the same time: `.planning/REQUIREMENTS.md` TRAN-05 says "the **three** encoding values"
while ROADMAP SC6 says "the **five**". Five is correct and REQUIREMENTS is amended to match. **D-20's
CONTEXT.md text also says three and is deliberately NOT edited** — CONTEXT.md is the locked record of
what was decided on 2026-09-02 and rewriting it to match a later correction destroys the audit trail.
The reconciliation is recorded in the script, in ROADMAP and in this plan's SUMMARY instead.

Discharges: D-19 (fail closed), D-20 (the standalone check's contract), **D-21 (settings "applied by
REST API, documented in `jellyfin.yaml`, ASSERTED IN THE CHECK" — the half of D-21 that was never
implemented)**, D-15/CONS-04 (assert-not-drifted is explicitly disclaimed as a firing claim).

Purpose: the observability layer this phase built to prevent a repeat currently cannot detect the
cheapest route back to the incident.
Output: an amended standing check with five fail-closed encoding assertions, five executed per-field
negative controls, a green-path-legible fold-in, and ROADMAP/REQUIREMENTS text that matches the code.
