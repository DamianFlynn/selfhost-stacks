<objective>
End the open question. Criterion 3's behavioural half has now been attempted with three capture
designs across three observation windows; this plan takes the result and either records that it is
closed, or hands the operator one prepared decision with its residual risk stated.

**This project exists because the pipeline was built three times and abandoned three times. A fourth
half-built attempt is the one outcome that must not happen.** So the exit is pre-committed: on a
non-PASS window the override that `04-VERIFICATION.md` § Gaps Summary already drafts is written,
**unsigned**, and put in front of the operator. The agent prepares it. The agent does not sign it.

**Why every gate in this plan is anchored to the frontmatter, and this is the whole of its
engineering.** `04-VERIFICATION.md` § Gaps Summary already contains the draft inside a fenced YAML
block whose keys sit at column 1. Measured at the parent commit: `grep -c '^overrides:'` returns
**1**, `accepted_by: "<developer name>"` is present at line 215 and `accepted_at: "<ISO timestamp>"`
at line 216. A file-wide presence gate would therefore be **satisfied by doing nothing at all**, and a
file-wide absence gate would be **unsatisfiable**, so a byte-proven PASS — the best outcome this phase
can reach — would end on a red gate. Both failure directions are the wrong way round. Every gate here
reads the frontmatter region only.
</objective>
