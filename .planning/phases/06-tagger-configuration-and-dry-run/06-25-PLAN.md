<objective>
Close **GC-08**, and settle the question it shares with GC-07.

GC-08 was recorded as *unverifiable from the diff supplied to the cross-family reviewer* — the
evidence was not supplied, the finding was not disputed. **It was verified during planning and the
factual premise holds**, at seven sites in `scripts/phase06-incremental-control.sh`: three
`/tmp/p6-man.*` files written with `>` inside `write_prog_manifest`, and `/tmp/p6-taghist.py`
written with `>` inside `write_prog_taghistory` and then **executed by `"$PY"` on the next line**.
All are fixed, predictable names in the container's `/tmp`.

The oracle uniquified its scratch filenames (IN-06) because *"the container's /tmp is world-writable
and these names used to be fixed … a pre-placed SYMLINK at a predictable name is followed by the
`>` redirections."* The sibling instrument, in the same gap-closure diff, running in the **same
container** as the same user, still has exactly that shape — and `/tmp/p6-taghist.py` is the worse
one, because a symlink pre-placed at that name redirects the write, and a regular file the redirect
cannot truncate leaves the **previous contents** to be executed.

**The finding this plan must actually resolve is the disagreement, not the filenames.** GC-07 says
the oracle's `RUN_TAG="$$"` comment asserts a security property the code does not have (PIDs are
small, sequential and enumerable; `$$` is the *workstation's* bash PID and has no relationship to
anything inside the container; and the step-1 probe that refuses any non-empty `$SCRATCH` is what
actually refuses a pre-placed name). GC-08 says the mitigation was applied to one of two siblings.
Either the threat is real, in which case it applies to both files, or it is not, in which case the
oracle's comment overstates it. The two are the same question asked from opposite ends, and this
round must not answer it twice differently.

Purpose: this plan owns the **incremental control's** half and **states the answer** that plan
06-27 then applies to the oracle's comment. One answer, two files, recorded in one artifact.

Output: temp names minted inside the container rather than written by the workstation, a
fail-closed branch when minting is unavailable, the two-arm proof untouched, and a stated
threat-model answer that 06-27 consumes.
